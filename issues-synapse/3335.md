I noticed the following the other morning (2018/05/31). Up until 08:50 UTC when erik restarted the event creator (and the join backlog was growing):

* event persistence was at 8Hz (12Hz after the restart)
* cpu usage on the master was at 75% (90% after the restart)
* avg db wait time was around 20ms (60ms after the restart, after a peak of 250ms)

The increase in event rate was almost entirely m.room.message from the freenode bridge.

So the question is: why was the cpu usage down at 75%. It could be db contention, except that the db wait time metric points the other way - so I suspect it's going to be something more like db latency - which might bite the irc bridge quite hard given we limit the amount of stuff we let it do in parallel. If the IRC bridge is trying to do more database-intensive work (like processing a lot of joins/parts), then that might cause a lower event throughput and lower CPU usage level than less database-intensive work.

It might be instructive to attempt to confirm or deny this hypothesis by looking at database transaction/scheduling time histograms, or by gathering metrics on the usage level of the db thread pool.

Another approach might be to look in more detail at these join requests that queue up for hours and see what is actually taking the time. Normally whenever I look at them, the majority complete very quickly once they finally get to the top of the queue - so some insights into what is bunging up the queue could be useful.
